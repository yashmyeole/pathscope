### Where the leaks come from

Near-duplicate matches for test queries by corpus source: {'pathvqa_train': 37}. Text matches by corpus source: {'pathvqa_train': 85}. 

**Every flagged pair is PathVQA-test ↔ PathVQA-train.** ARCH shares no image and no caption with PathVQA on any of the three checks — the two datasets come from different books and articles. So the leakage problem the guide warned about is real, but it is entirely internal to PathVQA's own split, which was evidently done at the Q/A-row level rather than the figure level.

### How the pHash threshold was chosen

16×16 DCT pHash → 256 bits; distance is Hamming. Every query/corpus nearest pair with d ≤ 86 was rendered and inspected by eye (`data/stats/contact_sheets/phase1_phash_pairs_part1..4.png`):

| Distance band | Pairs (test+dev) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| (-1.001, 24.0] | 25 | all the same photograph |
| (24.0, 48.0] | 24 | all the same photograph (crops, colour shifts) |
| (48.0, 58.0] | 8 | all the same photograph |
| (58.0, 70.0] | 8 | mixed — grey zone, adjudicated by hand |
| (70.0, 86.0] | 41 | mostly different; a few same (twins d=80, adrenal d=84) |
| (86.0, 100.0] | 1576 | different — chance level for 256-bit hashes |
| (100.0, 256.0] | 8 | different |

Automatic threshold **d ≤ 58** (zero false positives observed). Grey zone 59–86: 22 test pairs reviewed (7 same, 15 different); dev: 5 same, 22 different. Verdicts live in `manual_near_dups.csv` and are applied by `pathscope split`. The usual 'd ≤ 10' rule of thumb would have missed ~80% of the true duplicates here, because PathVQA's duplicates are re-photographed / re-cropped textbook figures, not byte-level copies.

Sensitivity (test queries at or below d): d≤12: 7, d≤24: 13, d≤48: 27, d≤58: 30, d≤70: 36, d≤86: 52.

### How the text check was designed

First attempt — `token_set_ratio` on the whole joined pseudo-caption — flagged 39% of test queries, almost all of them short diagnosis labels (`skin; skin kaposis sarcoma`) that two *different* images legitimately share. Removing those would strip out exactly the queries where retrieval has a chance to work and bias TCR downward. So the check is split in two:

* **Removed:** the query's longest gold fragment has ≥ 6 words and matches a corpus text at `token_sort_ratio` ≥ 85. These are the same figure legend or the same case photographed from another angle.
* **Kept, flagged `label_match`:** a short label identical to a corpus text — 248 test queries (28.9%). This becomes a stratification variable in Phase 4: TCR for queries whose exact diagnosis has a corpus exemplar vs. those without.

Sensitivity of the removed set to the minimum-words rule (test, score ≥ threshold): ≥6 words: 85, ≥8 words: 49, ≥10 words: 29; to the score threshold (≥6 words): ≥80: 90, ≥85: 85, ≥90: 81, ≥95: 78, ≥100: 77. The distribution is bimodal (identical or not), so the choice of 85 vs 95 moves <10 queries.

## Clean query set at a glance

744 test images; 237 with a label match in the corpus; median pseudo-caption length 9 words; 1 with empty gold text (kept, will be excluded from TCR by construction).

Corpus text length: ARCH captions median 20 words vs PathVQA-train pseudo-captions median 9 words. Phase 3/4 should report by corpus source — ARCH neighbours will carry roughly 3× more claims each.
